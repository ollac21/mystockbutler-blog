# Bloomberg Terminal alternatives for individual investors

If you buy a few companies a year, what you lack is not a Terminal. It is the reading: what the company sells, how much cash it really produces, and whether management did what it said. The Terminal is built for professionals who trade all day, with live prices on every market, news, chat and data on bonds and derivatives. Bloomberg does not publish its price; third-party estimates for 2025 and 2026 range from about $27,000 to $32,000 a year per seat.

Free tools such as Stock Analysis and Yahoo Finance give you the numbers. MyStockButler does the reading: one long report per company, written from its filings, every number traced to its source. Your first S&P 500 report is free: type a ticker below and sign up, no card.

## What you give up without a Terminal

Being honest about it: none of these tools replaces a Terminal for a professional. You give up live prices on every asset class, instant news, the chat network, bond and derivatives data, and trading from the same screen. If your job depends on those, the Terminal is worth its price.

For an individual investor buying a few companies a year, those features matter less than reading the company correctly.

## What you get instead, from our real reports

### "The facts are all over the place."

They are in one place. Our [Coca-Cola report](/stocks/ko/) runs eight chapters, 22,451 words and 54 tables, with 219 sources listed. A research hub sits beside each report, with the filings, call transcripts, management bios and interviews. McDonald's has 23 people cards and 8 call transcripts. Any report reads in French, German or Chinese (Hong Kong), on a click, in about 5 minutes.

### "How much of each dollar does it keep?"

Coca-Cola kept 28.7 cents of operating profit from each dollar of sales in 2025: operating income of $13,762 million on revenue of $47,941 million. Its own bottling business had $5,735 million of revenue but only $426 million of operating income, about 7 cents per dollar, a quarter of the company's average: one reason it sold most of its bottlers.

### "What is management saying now?"

You get the guidance in force, with its range, its period and the date it was given. [TSMC's](/stocks/tsm/earnings/) revenue guidance for the quarter to December 2026 is US$52.3 billion to US$54.3 billion, given on 15 October 2026.

### "Did management do what it said?"

Where management gives forecasts, we check them against results and score the record out of 5. [First Solar](/stocks/fslr/earnings/) forecast 2025 earnings of $17 to $20 a share and delivered $14.21. Its score is 1 of 5.

### "The CEO sold $17.5 million of stock. Should I worry?"

You get the trade and what it means, in plain words. McDonald's CEO sold 52,553 shares for about $17.5 million in February, under a pre-arranged trading plan. Our report reads it as planned personal selling, "not a stand-alone warning".

### "Results came out this morning. Is this already out of date?"

The 100 largest S&P 500 companies and the day's most talked-about stocks are redone first when they report, usually within about 5 hours. [Microsoft](/stocks/msft/) had a new report 1 hour 47 minutes after we saw its 28 October results.

### "A bank doesn't work like a soft-drink company."

We use a different set of tables for each of ten types of company, including banks, insurers, property trusts, oil companies and miners. A bank's report tracks interest margin, capital and loan losses. [JPMorgan's](/stocks/jpm/earnings/) shows its 2026 guidance for net interest income excluding Markets: $97,200 million.

### "What could it be worth?"

Every report ends with bear, base and bull share prices, each with its odds and assumptions. For Coca-Cola in October 2029: bear 54.72 (25% odds), base 83.68 (55%), bull 116.10 (20%). You never get a buy or sell call.

### "How do I know the numbers are right?"

Every number is traced to its source and every source is listed. AI models write the report, inside our own pipeline that builds the tables and checks the numbers, and a final check corrects or deletes statements it finds untrue. AI can still get a number wrong, so check the ones you rely on.

## Questions

### Is there a free alternative to the Bloomberg Terminal?

For researching a company, yes. Stock Analysis and Yahoo Finance show financial statements, ratios and news for free. For live professional data on every market, no free tool comes close.

### How much does a Bloomberg Terminal cost?

Bloomberg does not publish its price. Third-party estimates for 2025 and 2026 range from about $27,000 to $32,000 a year per seat, roughly $2,300 to $2,700 a month.

### Can an individual investor get a Bloomberg Terminal?

Bloomberg sells the Terminal on contract; ask Bloomberg whether it will contract with an individual. Most individual investors do not need one, because they use a fraction of what it does.

### What is the cheapest way to research one stock?

Free options exist: Stock Analysis and Yahoo Finance show the numbers, and Simply Wall St gives 5 company reports a month. Starter, at $30 a month, opens every S&P 500 report, and without a plan a report costs $5 (library) or $20 (new, ready in about 2 hours).

No. Our reports present facts, numbers and scenarios with their sources. They never tell you to buy or sell; the decision is yours.
