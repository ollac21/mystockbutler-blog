## What is Perplexity Finance, and what does it do?

It is the finance section of Perplexity, the AI search engine. Its finance page loaded only a title for us on 2 November 2026, so here is what Perplexity's own changelog said it held. The latest finance entries we could read are from June and July 2025, so check its finance page for what it has today:

- **SEC filings (June 2025).** You ask a question and it searches 10-K, 10-Q, S-1, S-4, 20-F and 8-K filings.
- **A stock screener (July 2025).** A Screener tab takes your filters in plain words.
- **Earnings documents (July 2025).** The Earnings tab gets a Documents section: the slides, quarterly reports and releases from the call you are viewing, free to every Perplexity user.
- **Analyst reports (July 2025).** A Research tab with non-paywalled analyst reports from around the web, next to Morningstar reports, also free to all users.

Each one answers a question you bring: what did the call say, what do the filings say, what do analysts think. You choose the question, and Perplexity finds the answer.

## What is Perplexity good at?

Give it its due. It's built to answer questions in your own words, with sources attached.

- **Any question, in your own words.** Its guide to researching an investment is a conversation: it starts with "What are the top 3 issues facing tesla? Give a bull case and a bear case for each." Then it keeps asking.
- **Sources attached.** When Perplexity opened SEC data to all investors on 5 June 2025, it wrote: "Every answer comes with direct citations to the source documents."
- **Calls, reports and news together.** Its guide says the answers draw on earnings calls, analyst coverage and news, with citations to recent transcripts, analyst reports and news sources.

If your question is about last week's news, or a single line in a filing, use Perplexity. We have no chat box and no news feed. We don't pretend to. If your question is "should I look at this company at all?", [type its ticker](#try) and read the eight chapters first.

## Is Perplexity Finance enough to research a stock?

Enough for the question you thought of. An answer is only as complete as what you asked.

Look at Perplexity's own guide to researching one stock. It lists eleven prompts on Tesla. The last asks, "what key metrics should I monitor quarterly". That is the whole problem in one line: the work is knowing what to ask.

- **No fixed structure.** The answer follows your question, so two companies get two different treatments unless you write the same eleven prompts twice.
- **The questions you didn't think of stay unasked.** Did management keep its promises? Is that insider selling real? Is free cash flow still positive once stock pay is subtracted?

Any chat, Perplexity or ChatGPT, answers what you ask. The report is for the day you don't know what to ask.
