# Is Seeking Alpha Premium worth it? What you get for one stock

Seeking Alpha Premium is worth it if you like reading many opinions on many stocks every week: $299 a year buys contributors' articles, quant ratings and AI summaries. It is a weaker fit for the moment you are about to put real money into one company. By then you have read the bull case and the bear case, and one question is still open: which one do the numbers support?

That is the job MyStockButler does. One long report per company, written from its filings, with every number traced to its source. Your first S&P 500 report is free: type a ticker below and sign up, no card.

## What Seeking Alpha Premium costs and gives you

Prices as shown on Seeking Alpha's pricing page on 26 October 2026.

- **Price:** $4.95 for the first month, then $299 a year. The pricing page shows no monthly plan. Seeking Alpha PRO costs $2,400 a year; the stock-picking service Alpha Picks is sold separately.
- **What you get:** articles written by contributors, quant ratings built on five factors (valuation, growth, profitability, momentum and earnings revisions), AI summaries, screeners.
- **What it does well:** many opinions, fast, on almost any US stock, and a lively comment section. If that is what you want, it is the better choice.
- **What it leaves to you:** an article is one contributor's view. Deciding which view to trust, and checking its numbers against the company's own filings, is your job.

## What you still want to know before you buy

These are the questions we built MyStockButler to answer. Each answer below comes from one of our real reports.

### "Which of these articles do I believe?"

Start from what the company itself reported. Every number in our reports is traced to its source and every source is listed: 219 of them in our [Coca-Cola report](/stocks/ko/), which runs eight chapters and 22,451 words. AI models write the report, inside our own pipeline that builds the tables and checks the numbers, and a final check corrects or deletes statements it finds untrue. AI can still get a number wrong, so check the ones you rely on.

### "Did management do what it said?"

Where management gives forecasts, we check them against results and score the record out of 5. [First Solar](/stocks/fslr/earnings/) forecast 2025 earnings of $17 to $20 a share and delivered $14.21. Its score is 1 of 5.

### "The CEO sold $17.5 million of stock. Should I worry?"

You get the trade and what it means, in plain words. McDonald's CEO sold 52,553 shares for about $17.5 million in February, under a pre-arranged trading plan. Our report reads it as planned personal selling, "not a stand-alone warning".

### "Results came out this morning. Is this already out of date?"

The 100 largest S&P 500 companies and the day's most talked-about stocks are redone first when they report, usually within about 5 hours. [Microsoft](/stocks/msft/) had a new report 1 hour 47 minutes after we saw its 28 October results.

### "I've never heard of this company."

The reports are written for you. Terms are explained as they come up and a line under each table says what it means. Coca-Cola's report tells you how the business works: the company creates demand for the brand, "while a network of bottlers often makes, packages and delivers the drink". Filings, call transcripts, management bios and interviews sit beside the report in one research hub. Banks, insurers, property trusts, oil companies and miners get their own tables, and any report reads in French, German or Chinese (Hong Kong), on a click, in about 5 minutes.

### "What could it be worth?"

Every report ends with bear, base and bull share prices, each with its odds and assumptions. Coca-Cola's weighted case is a 2% loss by October 2029, dividends included. You never get a buy or sell call: the decision stays yours.
