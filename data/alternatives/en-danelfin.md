## Danelfin review: what does it cost and what do you get?

Danelfin's plans, billed yearly, as shown on its pricing pages on 2 November 2026:

- **Free:** 10 stock and ETF reports a month.
- **Plus, $22 a month.** Pro, $59 a month. Elite, $134 a month.

What the paid plans add, from Danelfin's own blog:

- **Plus and Pro:** one-year price forecasts and trade ideas (post of 24 October 2025).
- **Elite** (launched 1 April 2026): everything in Pro, plus unlimited trade ideas, short positions included, unlimited portfolios, a file of daily AI Scores back to 2017, API access and priority support.

**The AI Score.** It runs from 1 ("strong sell") to 10 ("strong buy"), in Danelfin's own words, and it is tuned to a three-month horizon. Its own disclaimer says the scores are probabilities worked out from past market behavior and don't predict actual results. It covers US stocks and ETFs, and European stocks.

## Is Danelfin accurate?

Danelfin's own audit says its top-rated stocks beat the market. Its post of 14 April 2026 reads: "Stocks with the highest AI Scores have consistently outperformed the market." That is Danelfin's claim, from the company that sells the score. The audit covers the scores it gave US stocks since 2017. We did not test it.

## What does Danelfin do well?

It ranks fast. One number per stock, on one scale, so you can sort a long list in minutes. It publishes an audit of its own record. For the job of finding five names among a hundred, a score is the right tool.

## Why look for a Danelfin alternative?

Because the score ends where your real question starts.

**"The score says 9. Why?"** A score is a probability. It isn't an explanation. Take Amazon. A report can tell you things a three-month probability can't. Our [Amazon report](/stocks/amzn/) shows 2025 free cash flow of $7,695 million, which turns into minus $11,772 million once the $19,467 million of shares paid to staff comes off. It scores management's own forecasts against results: 5 out of 5 on Amazon. It counts 299 insider filings in twelve months, 129 of them open-market sales, and says which are real selling and which are pay mechanics.

**"What has to go right?"** One number about the next three months can't say. A report can. VICI's forecast has three cases and their chances: bear 27%, base 55%, bull 18%, to three dates. The weighted price is $24.76 in October 2027, against $23.52 on 29 October, a 13% return with dividends. See it in [VICI's forecast](/stocks/vici/). No buy or sell call. Danelfin's scale runs to "strong buy". We never make that call.

**"What does management say?"** A research hub sits beside each report: management bios, interview links, the last 8 earnings-call transcripts, insider tables. [TSMC's report](/stocks/tsm/) comes with 22 interview and appearance links for 6 executives, such as its CFO Wendell Huang on Yahoo Finance, 15 October 2026.

A score answers one question: will this stock beat the market? A report answers the ones you didn't think to ask. It has the same eight chapters every time, so two companies sit side by side: the business, its market, history and capital decisions, management and ownership, the numbers, valuation, what comes next, and where the price could go. Every number traced to its source. Click, read the filing, decide.

It's a report, not a chat. You can't ask follow-up questions, and it sends no live alerts. Any report reads in French, German or Chinese (Hong Kong) on a click.

A note on the AI: it writes the reports, inside a pipeline of ours that builds the tables and checks the numbers. A final check fixes or deletes the statements it finds untrue. A number can still slip through, so check the ones you rely on.
