## What do you get with WallStreetZen?

- **Zen Ratings.** A letter grade on each stock, from a model WallStreetZen says weighs 115 factors, one of them an AI factor. A is "Strong Buy", B "Buy", C "Hold", D "Sell" and F "Strong Sell". It says stocks rated A have averaged a 28.50% annual return since 2006. That's WallStreetZen's own figure, from the company that sells the rating.
- **A stock page with checks.** Each stock has a page with component grades (momentum, financials, value, growth and more) and a list of due diligence checks. On its TSMC page, 35 more checks sit behind Premium.
- **Premium extras.** The current list of A-rated stocks, premium screeners, an AI score for each stock, forecasts from top analysts, a one-sentence note on why a price moved, and email updates for your watchlist.

That's the grade. For the reasons behind a stock, [type its ticker](#try): your first S&P 500 report is free, no card.

## What are WallStreetZen's pros and cons?

We read WallStreetZen's pages and didn't test its ratings. This is what its pages say, not how they perform.

## What does a Zen Rating leave you to find out?

Here is one stock on both sides. On 2 November 2026, WallStreetZen's [TSMC](/stocks/tsm/) page showed:

- A Zen Rating of **B**, labelled Buy.
- A statement that TSMC trades above an intrinsic value of **$147.04**, from an updated version of Benjamin Graham's formula, against a price of **$406.13** (updated 30 October).

One grade. One value. They answer a question: how does this stock rank, and what does a formula say it's worth? They don't tell you what you'd be betting on.

Our TSMC report, written on 18 October, answers that. It doesn't give one value. It gives three cases, with their chances, to three dates. For October 2027: bear $316.21 at 25%, base $484.67 at 55%, bull $613.62 at 20%. The probability-weighted price is $468.34, a 15% return with dividends. By October 2029 it is $742.49.

And it says what has to go right or wrong. The bull case needs strong AI demand, better yields on TSMC's two-nanometer chips and fuller advanced-packaging capacity. The bear case is weaker plant utilization and the cost of ramping new capacity. No buy or sell call. You decide.

We're not telling you $147 is wrong or $468 is right. The two answer different questions. A grade tells you how a formula ranks the stock. A report tells you what has to be true.

Premium is $1 for a 14-day trial, then $234 a year, with a 90-day money-back guarantee. ### Why not just ask ChatGPT?

A chat answers what you think to ask. The TSMC report puts three price cases with their chances (25%, 55%, 20%) and 407 sources on the page without a prompt. A chat can answer your follow-up; we can't.

### What is a Zen Rating?

WallStreetZen's letter grade for a stock, from A ("Strong Buy") to F ("Strong Sell"), from a model that it says weighs 115 factors. WallStreetZen reports that stocks rated A have averaged a 28.50% annual return since 2006. That is its own figure.

### Is WallStreetZen worth it?

Yes, for screening by grade, with analyst forecasts, at $234 a year. On its own, no, for a decision on one company: a grade doesn't give you the business, the management, the cash or the price cases. That's what a written report is for.
